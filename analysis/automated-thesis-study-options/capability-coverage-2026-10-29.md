# Capability and evidence map — 29 October 2026

This is a coverage map, not a claim that every situation is tested. Original studies, failures and denominators remain unchanged. The 252-conversation population below is the explicitly labeled post-hoc reconstruction from three execution phases; live catalog data can vary between phases.

| Capability | Existing automated evidence | Remaining boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retrieval and multi-need | Five event groups and ten task labels in the July-derived 150; live public reads in 45 scenarios and controlled empty results in five. Some cases assert candidate and delivered link. | A returned candidate is not independently verified as relevant or available. Empty fixtures do not establish a real catalog has no match. |
| Provider facts in responses | `artifacts/bounded-coverage-2026-10-29/summary.json`: 372 detected numbered sections treated as candidate cards on origin-verified turns; 366 bound to exactly one same-turn trace record, six unassessable. Of 343 detected price-level fields, 337 matched and six lacked assessable evidence; 29 not detected. Location string was exact for 366, six unassessable. Of 30 detected explicit minimum-price numbers, all 30 matched; no minimum-price phrase was detected in 342 cards. | Normalized same-turn traces are not independently archived marketplace truth; this is consistency, not verified factual truth. No parser covers all prose, currencies, categories, negation, available capacity or services. Location non-exact is not automatically incorrect. Cards and turns are not independent users. |
| Provider choice | In 15 reconstructed July-derived first-option selection conversations, 14 linked the uniquely identified first card to the selected ID; one was unassessable. New validation has 15 second-option cases across five event groups, three per scenario. | Any ambiguous ordinal, missing unique provider source or missing verified response is unassessable; results may fail closed. Do not infer first-choice correctness for unassessable cases. |
| Quotes / event date | Existing master completion case checked two simulated provider/date receipts: one of three passed in the prior current follow-up. New validation has one- and two-provider cases, each repeated three times, with exact selected IDs, 2028 event date, fixture effect counts and no duplicate on recall. | Simulated writes are not proof real providers were contacted or business results occurred. |
| FAQ / customer support | In the original 102 executed cases of the expanded run, all 24 prespecified FAQ requested-value assertions passed; other support cases include purchase identity and dispute handling. Separate calculator target had 3/3 source-link passes. | Literal value/link presence cannot prove correct association, non-contradiction, full factual faithfulness or satisfaction; source article retrieval is not itself proof of a correct answer. |
| Controlled provider details | New three-repeat case checks provider name, location, category, minimum-price text, absence of one other-provider price and no side effects against a known synthetic fixture. | Literal presence and absence are bounded; they do not fully parse claims, currency or negation, or establish real marketplace accuracy. |
| Auth, continuity, RSVP, support | Existing hard checks include no unauthorized effects, privacy refusal, RSVP idempotence and returning to a question after takeover. OTP-specific cases were excluded from the expanded planned population per user preference. | Authentication and real customer/backend behavior are outside these mocked scenarios; no user satisfaction or broad coverage is claimed. |

The deterministic detector in `src/evals/bounded-response-checks.ts` compares only named output forms against the same-turn trace. It reports unmatched/ambiguous records separately and does not replace historical scores. Reproduce with `npx tsx scripts/analyze-bounded-response-evidence.ts` and inspect the per-card and per-selection records. Automated parsing decisions can themselves be wrong outside their narrow grammars. No human labeling or model judge was used.

The one unassessable first-option comparison was `study.social.05` repetition 1: neither the source turn nor the selection turn retained provider candidates or a provider URL. It was already a hard failure in the original study for not recording a selected provider; the derivative identity check does not fabricate a target or overwrite that failure.
